# Summary Report: "FCAJ x Agentic AI Build Week - Show Up. Build. Pitch. WIN!"

### Event Objectives

- Learn about the product-building journeys of teams participating in Agentic AI Build Week
- Understand how Agentic AI, AWS, and business thinking can be combined to solve real-world problems
- Gain experience in identifying pain points, controlling scope, building an MVP, and pitching during a hackathon

### Main Content

#### 1. Opening and Development Direction

AWS representatives emphasized that AI and agentic systems are changing the speed of product development. Young people need to learn continuously, experiment with ideas, and view technology as a tool for creating practical value. A hackathon evaluates more than technical ability; it also helps participants develop innovation, collaboration, and solution-presentation skills.

#### 2. Conversational AI for Food Ordering

One Team introduced an AI system that accepts conversational orders across multiple channels. The solution standardizes messages, identifies customer intent, and routes data to the appropriate agents. The presentation showed that an AI product must simultaneously address user experience, accuracy, operational workflows, and integration capability.

#### 3. Multi-Agent Business Strategy Analysis

Signal Scout presented a solution that collects and connects publicly available business data, then uses multiple agents to analyze operating models, strategies, and their applicability to other organizations. The team emphasized that identifying the right pain point, building an MVP clear enough to demonstrate, and explaining business value are more important than merely developing a complex architecture.

#### 4. AI-Generated AWS Architecture and Infrastructure as Code

Another team developed an assistant for Solution Architects. Users can enter requirements in natural language and upload internal documents or policies; the system then recommends an architecture diagram, estimates costs, and generates Terraform/Infrastructure as Code. The solution can potentially shorten design time, but it still requires validation, guardrails, and human approval before deployment.

#### 5. Computer Vision for Crowd-Flow Monitoring

Team 3K introduced a system that uses cameras and the YOLO model to detect and monitor crowd density at airports or supermarkets. Data is presented on a dashboard, and an agent can recommend actions when an area is at risk of congestion. The team also discussed practical challenges involving network connectivity, cloud costs, model selection, and limiting project scope within 24 hours.

#### 6. Agentic AI for Anti-Money Laundering Investigations

Team Sixer presented an Adaptive Workflow Engine for banks and financial institutions. The system combines abnormal-transaction detection with multiple agents that review customer records, fund flows, and sanctions lists. Investigation results, evidence, and reasoning are retained to support audits, while serious cases continue to be escalated to human reviewers for final decisions.

### Lessons Learned

- Begin with the problem and target users, not the technology
- Define in-scope and out-of-scope items clearly to complete an MVP within a limited timeframe
- A working demo that communicates value clearly is often more persuasive than an oversized idea that cannot yet be implemented
- Assign roles early, communicate clearly, and set aside personal ego to create a strong teamwork foundation
- Agentic AI systems require guardrails, logging, output validation, and human-in-the-loop controls
- Hackathons also provide portfolio material, pitching experience, and professional networking opportunities

### Application to Study and Work

- Practice using Amazon Bedrock, AWS Step Functions, Lambda, DynamoDB, and suitable monitoring services
- Prepare slides, documentation, a demo plan, and contingency options before presenting
